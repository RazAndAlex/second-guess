# Places to look

Used in step 1 of `SKILL.md`. These are links only, and this toolkit ships no data. Each line says what the place holds. Nothing here is a source until you open a page and make a card from it.

**How to read the marks.** All marks date from 2026-11-05, from one plain automated request to the front page.

- **opened:** the page answered and its title matched. We read the title only, so what the line says it holds is our own description.
- **not opened:** the site refused the request or returned no title. Your harness may still reach it. The link itself is what we believe is right, and we have not confirmed it.

A site that blocks automated requests can leave a card `unchecked`, so prefer open sources when two places cover the same fact. Paywalled sites often end the same way.

## Earlier cases, as datasets

These can reach `stats` as a CSV file in `idea-test-files/`.

- https://webrobots.io/kickstarter-datasets/ (opened): monthly files of Kickstarter projects, with funded or failed. It answers one narrow question: would this get funded on Kickstarter? Ended projects in these files favour funded ones, because projects vanish between files, so read the file's notes before you use it.
- https://www.kaggle.com/datasets (opened): datasets uploaded by users. Quality varies, and a download may need an account.
- https://huggingface.co/datasets (opened): datasets uploaded by users, mostly for machine learning.
- https://zenodo.org/ (opened): open files attached to research, with a named author and a date.
- https://dataverse.harvard.edu/ (not opened, answered with no title): research datasets deposited by authors.
- https://catalog.data.gov/dataset (opened): United States government datasets.
- https://data.europa.eu/ (opened): European public datasets.

## How businesses and markets fared

- https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/bds.html (opened): United States figures on how many firms start, close and survive.
- https://www.bls.gov/bdm/ (not opened, refused): United States figures on how long business establishments last.
- https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/data/database (opened): European statistics, including business survival.
- https://data.worldbank.org/ (opened): country-level indicators.
- https://data.oecd.org/ (not opened, refused): indicators across wealthy countries.
- https://ourworldindata.org/ (opened): charts with their sources named.
- https://www.pewresearch.org/ (opened): survey reports on how people use technology and services.
- https://www.statista.com/ (opened): market charts. Many sit behind a paywall, so follow each one to its named source.
- https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/ (not opened, refused): annual filings of United States public companies, a place to read a rival's own words.
- https://clinicaltrials.gov/ (opened): registered clinical trials, for health ideas.

## Competitors and substitutes

- https://github.com/search (opened): open-source projects that do the same job. Stars and last update show whether a project is alive.
- https://www.producthunt.com/ (not opened, refused): recently launched products.
- https://alternativeto.net/ (not opened, refused): lists of products that replace a named product.
- https://www.g2.com/ (not opened, refused): business software listings with user reviews.
- https://www.trustpilot.com/ (not opened, refused): user reviews of companies.
- https://apps.apple.com/ (opened): apps for iPhone, with ratings.
- https://play.google.com/store (opened): apps for Android, with ratings.
- https://patents.google.com/ (opened): patents, which show who has worked on the same idea.
- https://www.crunchbase.com/ (opened): company profiles. Much of it needs an account.

## What people say

- https://hn.algolia.com/ (opened): search across Hacker News, for people's own reports on a product or a kind of product.
- https://news.ycombinator.com/ (opened): the same site's threads, to read a discussion in full.
- https://www.reddit.com/ (opened): forums by topic. It often refuses automated requests.
- https://trends.google.com/trends/ (not opened, answered with no title): how interest in a search term changed over time.

## Research papers

- https://openalex.org/ (opened): an open catalog of research papers.
- https://www.semanticscholar.org/ (opened): search for research papers.
- https://arxiv.org/ (opened): preprints, which have not yet been through review.
- https://www.nber.org/papers (opened): economics working papers.
- https://www.ssrn.com/ (not opened, refused): preprints in business and social science.

## Tools for the steps

- https://web.archive.org/ (opened): old copies of pages. Use it to find a page that has moved. The program tries it by itself in step 2.
- https://www.gjopen.com/faq (opened): how a forecasting site writes a question that can be checked. Read it for step 5 when you draft a target.
