You can lose months and money building the wrong thing.

Ask an AI first, and it may agree with you.

It can even invent a quote.

So here the AI has to argue, and code looks up what it says.

Your agent, your model, your plan

It runs on the agent you already have: Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Gemini CLI, GitHub Copilot, Windsurf, Cline, Goose, OpenCode, Zed, Amp, Warp, Kimi Code CLI and Roo Code go in through the same open installer.

Run for real so far: Claude Code and Codex. The agent needs web search and must be able to run Python. second-guess ships no data, no model, no API key and no purchase number.

The AI looks

1AI

Find data

The agent lists what must be true for the idea to work, then looks for evidence. Here is one real idea from one real run.

“A subscription of refill tablets for household cleaning sprays, posted to homes in Italy every two months.”

  • People run out every two months
  • Plastic waste bothers them enough
  • They already pay for sprays
  • They would mix it themselves
  • They would keep a fixed delivery
  • They can be reached online
  • Postage fits a small order
  • Tablets clean well enough
  • Rivals do not already do it better
  • Labels and green claims hold up

The AI attacks

4AI

Attack

Six simulated people, hostile by design. Each says what kills the idea and gives you a question for a real person. They are AI role-play, and their words prove nothing.

My refill goes in the trolley with the pasta and the milk, without a thought. This idea swaps that for a sign-up, a parcel, a drawer of tablets and a new small job, and gives me nothing the trolley does not.

Ask a real one: Think about the last time your spray bottle ran out. Walk me through what you did next, from seeing it empty to having the new one in your hand.

One home uses very little spray and a tablet pack lasts a long time, so a parcel on a fixed rhythm is mostly a parcel nobody needs. The cupboard fills, the customer feels guilty and cancels. A thin order of one small thing also cannot carry its postage; I make delivery pay only with a big basket across laundry, dishwasher and kits.

Ask a real one: Think about the last group of customers who joined a refill subscription on a first-order offer. What happened to their orders over the following months, and what did you change afterwards?

The spray is already in the trolley because it was on offer, at a price I can see on the shelf. A box once kept charging me and took several emails to stop, so the word subscription already closes the page. A bill that lands every other month is a bill I forget.

Ask a real one: Think back to the last thing you signed up for that arrived at home on a schedule. Did you keep it, change it or stop it, how did you stop it, and where did you get that thing afterwards?

The tablet brings back the one job I switched to ready-made sprays to avoid, and the bottle runs empty at the wrong moment. My water is hard. An eco cleaner once streaked my glass and left limescale in the shower, and I went back to the strong brand. If the first bottle streaks, there is no second.

Ask a real one: Think about the last time you tried a cleaner that was not your usual brand. What happened on the glass and the shower with your tap water, and what did you get the next time you ran out?

The customer finishes making the product at home, so the liquid on the shelf sits in a bottle with no warning once the leaflet is in the bin. A child or a grandmother finding that bottle is the case that brings an inquiry. Green claims must also survive a whole-life check that includes a parcel to each house, or the claim goes and the subscription loses its reason to exist.

Ask a real one: Think of the last cleaning product you reviewed that the customer had to dilute or mix at home. What did you require on the label and leaflet, and what happened once the customer put the liquid in their own bottle?

Vinegar, bicarbonate and a cloth cover my cleaning, and the corner shop has the degreaser. A parcel once vanished with a neighbour, and a subscription a grandchild set up could not be stopped. An online account, a card and a postman are all things I avoid.

Ask a real one: Think about the last time you ran out of a cleaning product at home. What did you do that same day to get more, and where did you get it?

20:42:04

15%
The AI gave its lean. Spray tablets ask shoppers to add water themselves, and these chains already stock bottle refills that ask for no new habit. This is the AI’s opinion, and the report says so.
